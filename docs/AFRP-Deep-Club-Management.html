Running a club

v1.0 · 20 Aug 2026 · the officer’s year · every moment paired

Eighteen clubs, run entirely by volunteers who have day jobs and change every year or two. Club management is where the federation’s design meets its hardest constraint: whoever does this work does it in the evening, and will hand it to someone else sooner than you think. Every mechanism below is judged on how well it survives a handover — including the handovers that go badly.

The cycle that repeats forever

The officer year — election, grants, handover

Everything about club management is downstream of one fact: the people doing it are temporary. The design’s answer is that authority is granted, scoped and dated — never assumed, never inherited by knowing a password.

1The club elects its officersclub rules · R42
The club

Under the club’s own governing rules — separately incorporated, own bylaws. The federation does not run club elections and the platform does not pretend to.

The federation

What the federation needs is the affiliation form: who holds which office, from when. That filing is what turns an election result into access.

2Grants are issuedR39
Federation staff

Each office becomes a (person, role, scope) grant, scoped to this club only and end-dated to the term. Five sensitive permissions always require exact grants — no role inherits them.

The new treasurer

She can see Detroit’s dues, Detroit’s roster, Detroit’s remittance — and nothing of any other club, and nothing of the federation. Her scope is visible to her, so she knows what she is trusted with.

Roles & permissions
3The handoverR39 · S5
Outgoing officer

The treasury reconciles and both officers sign the reconciliation. The outgoing officer’s grants end on a date — not when someone remembers, and not when trust runs out.

Incoming officer

He inherits a signed statement of what he is taking on, rather than a shoebox and a verbal assurance.

The bad handover: if the outgoing officer will not sign — or has died — the reconciliation is filed unilaterally with a stated discrepancy and escalates to the federation. The grants still end on their date. An unsigned handover is a fact to record, never a reason to leave access open indefinitely.

Twelve times a year

The treasurer’s month

The single most consequential volunteer job in the federation, and the one most likely to go quiet.

1The remittance statement arrivesR21
What the federation sends

Dues collected nationally on the club’s behalf are a liability until remitted. The statement shows mode, gross, due, and carried — the four numbers that let a treasurer reconcile in ten minutes instead of an evening.

What the treasurer does

She checks it against her own book, accepts it, and the money moves. Where it disagrees, the disagreement is a queue item with a named owner, not an email thread.

Remittance
2A member renews mid-monthS3
The ledger

Gross posting, three journal lines. If the payment reverses after the remittance has gone out, the reversal creates a receivable on the club’s next statement — never a negative liability and never a quiet adjustment to a closed month.

The member

His standing reverts with an effective date, and any roll frozen in between stays frozen with an annotation. Nothing is recomputed behind him.

The federation’s close

Postings queue with full detail; the national daily close never waits on one club’s dead token. September’s re-auth replays the queue idempotently — same date+fund+kind keys, no duplicates.

The club sees “August queued, replayed 4 Sep”. What it must not see is a silent gap that surfaces in November as a missing month.

The membership secretary’s queue

The unglamorous work that everything else depends on.

1New members and transfersR1 · R2 · R34
The secretary

National and club memberships are separate peer records that never merge. A transfer is not an administrative move: relocation prompts the member, and the member acts.

A member moving cities

She moves from Cleveland to Houston and chooses what happens to each membership. Nobody bulk-moves her, and her Cleveland membership runs to its own expiry if she wants it to.

2Multi-club membersR2 · R16
The registry view

Overlap is shown, never merged. For federal apportionment a member counts once, through the club of record pointer, and attested counts are deduplicated against it before any weight is computed.

Samira, who belongs to two

She votes in each club’s own elections where she is current, and counts exactly once federally. Three memberships, three lapse clocks, one person.

Club registry
3Roster freshness on the manual doorR42
A manual-roster club

Clubs choose their integration door: live feed, dated manual roster, or platform-native after cutover. All three are first-class and labelled at every point of use.

What it costs to be late

A manual roster older than the record-date window is stale — visible on the health screen well before it becomes a credentialing refusal.

Coming onto the platform

Eighteen clubs, eighteen different starting points — Breeze, a spreadsheet, a shoebox, nothing at all. The migration wizard exists because “which club goes first” had no screen, and the answer must always be the club decides.

1Stages 1–3R3 · R42
Federation and club together

Full import with identity resolution — candidate duplicates to the merge queue, never auto-merged, nothing written back to the club’s system. Reconciliation to the cent with the treasurer. Then a one-way nightly delta sync the club can pause on indefinitely.

The club’s members

Nothing about their membership changes during any of this. The club’s system stays the club’s system until the club votes otherwise.

Migration
2Before cutover — the verification outreachS1
What the red team forced

Imported emails and phones are unverified channels, and S1 forbids money, PII or consent across them. So before the cutover vote, each member verifies a channel front-door style; the unverified land on a hygiene queue with a postal fallback.

The member

He confirms his own email once, and from then on the platform can actually be trusted to reach him — which matters most at ballot time.

3CutoverR42 · R43
The club’s vote

The club votes under its own rules. Only then does the platform become its system of record, effective-dated, with the registry gaining its third door value: platform-native.

What changes

An electorate frozen mid-migration reads the door of record as of the record date — migration state is part of the snapshot’s label, so nothing shifts under a frozen roll.

The states nobody designs for

No officers, going dormant, coming back

Real federations have clubs that go quiet. A design that only handles healthy clubs fails exactly when it is needed, so these states are specified rather than discovered.

1A club with no officersR17 · R42
What the federation can see

The club is still visible in the registry. What it cannot do is certify delegates — surfaced plainly on the health screen, not hidden until convention.

What members keep

Members keep their club memberships and everything that comes with them. A leadership gap is not a membership penalty.

2DormancyS4 · R43 · S3
An effective-dated status

Dormancy is effective-dated: memberships run to their own expiry — no mid-term cancellation — and prepaid dues stay a liability, remitted on reactivation or refunded through the reversal model. Never silently absorbed.

A multi-club member

If her club of record goes dormant she is prompted to redesignate a current club. Only a member with no other current club falls to the at-large roll at the next record date.

3Coming backR43
Reactivation

Reactivation is another effective-dated status change with a Board action behind chartering decisions.

What does not move

Frozen rolls are never recomputed. A club going dormant on 20 October changes future record dates only — history stays exactly as it was.

What federation staff may never do. Staff configure a club: charter, officers, money, integration door, electorate. Staff never edit a member’s club membership record directly — corrections happen at source, so the club stays the authority on its own roster and every downstream reader sees the same fact with the same label.
