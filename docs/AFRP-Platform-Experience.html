The Platform Experience — sixteen journeys and the progression they ride, red-teamed and settled

v1.1 · 19 Aug 2026 · two red-team rounds: 157 agents · 102 attacks · 88 refuted · 19 confirmed & settled

Sixteen detailed user experiences — joining, renewing across multiple clubs, a daughter turning eighteen, a death in the family, the convention from both sides of the desk, a scholarship cycle, a camp season, a treasurer’s year, the magazine freeze, a committee seat, federation club management, how the directory works, and a professional identity evolving over fifteen years — each written as a concrete worked example and then attacked by an adversarial red team, in two rounds. Between the principles and the journeys sits the piece that ties them together: the integrated progression — four rails of programs that hand off to each other, convened yearly by the Convention and continuously by the clubs. 88 of 102 attacks were refuted, which is the spec holding; the 19 that survived are settled inline — amber steps mark what the red team changed.

16 journeys · every module exercised the integrated progression · 4 rails 157 agents · 2 rounds · adversarial verification 19 breaks confirmed — all settled principles S1–S7

S1 · Verified channel first

No money is captured, no PII is emitted, and no consent flips across a channel that has not been verified as belonging to that person. Join, login, and the 18th-birthday refresh collapse into ONE front-door surface: verify the channel, then create; verify the adult’s own channel, then refresh. Born from breaks in J01, J03 and J07.

S2 · One life-event state machine

Every life event runs claimed → verified-pending → released-per-surface. Operational effects (billing suspension, storefront/relay stop, assignment freeze) fire at verified-pending and are reversible; publications wait for the approval gate. The reporter is never in their own approval set; death claims need an evidence artifact; an explicit family objection is a blocking veto with a defined window; an empty approval set has a named exit. Born from the five J04 breaks.

S3 · One reversal model

A refund or chargeback that crosses a remittance or entity boundary creates a receivable — never a negative liability or negative restricted class. Standing changes take an effective date; frozen snapshots are never recomputed but carry a “payment reversed post-freeze” annotation that downstream checks must honor by rule. One model for dues, gifts, store and conduit money. Born from breaks in J02, J04 and J12.

S4 · Weights fix when voting opens

Floor extraction re-divides delegate weights only between motions: when voting opens on a motion, weights freeze for that ballot. And a national member with no club counts federally through an at-large roll, so “counts exactly once” is true for everyone — both drafted as bylaw amendments for the Board, since 9.1.3 is real law. Born from the J05 and cross-journey breaks.

S5 · Corrections are held, never lost

A member dispute filed before a copy freeze blocks that entry (not the issue) until resolved — an entry in dispute prints as held, not as wrong. Downstream archives (Preservation Project) ingest only consent-classed material; print requires its own switch, and the archive honors it. Born from the J09 breaks.

S6 · Refusals name the rule, not the relatives

A conflict-of-interest block shows “recused · rule 41” — never which relationship triggered it, because the refusal must not leak the tree. And Board budget approvals are allocations; only donor designation creates restriction — the words on the screen now say which is which. Born from the J10 breaks.

S7 · Suppression must survive composition

An aggregate that cannot survive arithmetic is a person-level disclosure wearing a report’s clothes. Pipeline and funnel reports are frozen periodic snapshots (the rule-43 pattern) with complementary suppression: a cell displays only when the cell and its within-margin complement both clear the floor — across tabs, across successive snapshots, and against the platform’s own public surfaces (award stages, reunion rosters). Ad-hoc cross-tabs that would imply an under-floor cell are refused, naming the rule. Born from the J16 and Pathways breaks in round two.

The integrated progression — how the programs fit together

The platform’s deepest claim is not any one screen — it is that the programs hand off to each other. Four rails carry a member from camp to career, from tree to magazine, from gift to Ramallah; the Convention convenes every rail once a year and the clubs convene them continuously. The journeys below are these rails experienced one person at a time.

How a handoff works

Invitation, never enrollment

A handoff fires when an enrollment record hits a milestone (a camper ages out, a scholar graduates) — as a program invitation, a consented channel class of its own: recruitment is not an operational notice, so it never rides the operational rail past a marketing opt-out. The member accepts or ignores; declining is undated-neutral — no committee ever sees a "declined" flag.

Windows, not gates

Eligibility is ages and stages, not prerequisites. A 27-year-old who never camped enters Project Hope directly — the rails are the common path, never the only path.

Aggregates, not surveillance

The cross-program funnel report is owned by no single committee — the executive director’s office is its data custodian — and publishes annually, to the Board and the convention program, as a frozen snapshot with complementary suppression (S7): it never becomes a person-level list, and no arithmetic across its tabs, editions, or the stage roster makes it one.

Entities stay entities

An invitation crossing an entity boundary (AFRP → an ARFHSN program) carries contact and eligibility only. Scope rules travel with the data (rule 39); the entity crossing itself rides a standing data-sharing agreement, because rule 39’s scopes end at the federation’s edge.

The Pathways model was attacked too

Round two sent a dedicated breaker at the progression itself — five attacks refuted (entity handoffs, death races, rescinded milestones, the consent void at eighteen, non-member invitations all held), one confirmed:

criticalFunnel drill-down deanonymized by the convening layer's public stage data
The Toledo drill-down publishes a 100% cell — alumni 5, applied 5 — so no small cell fires, yet every identifiable camp-reunion attendee is disclosed as a scholarship applicant. PATHWAYS's own convening layer then mandates "scholarship awards on stage": 4 consenting Toledo winners are named, and subtraction deterministically exposes the fifth as the rejected applicant — reconstructing the suppressed awarded=4 and rejected=1 cells. Floor-only suppression ("stops at the suppression floor") cannot protect margin-equal cells or complements recoverable from the platform's own mandated person-lev…Settled: Settled under S7: a drilled cell displays only if both the cell AND its within-margin complement clear the floor — which kills 100% cells and small stage-to-stage drops; failing cells roll up to region or at-large. Stage recognition stays per-instance consent, and the consent screen now says what the stage plus the funnel could jointly reveal.

criticalReporter self-approval: one person can fabricate and publish a death
Because a one-degree reporter is inside the family-approval set and a single approval releases, one person can fabricate a death (desk checks identity, never the death), approve it herself, and trigger immediate irreversible fan-out — RBPN takedown, reviewer seat vacated, tree closure, billing cancellation, In Memoriam queued — while the spec defines no un-release and no precedence for a family objection that arrives after the approval lands.Settled: Exclude the reporter from the approval set (or require a second, distinct approver for death). Make an explicit objection a blocking veto for a defined window rather than a parallel escalation, and define release as the LATER of approval and objection-window expiry. Require the desk to attach at least one external evidence artifact (obituary, funeral-home notice) before deceased-pending can be approved.
criticalRBPN takedown and reviewer-seat vacation are wrongly gated behind the publication approval
Step 4 gates the RBPN takedown and reviewer-seat vacation behind family approval of the announcement even though the spec itself calls the takedown a non-publication and fires other operational effects at deceased-pending, so an objection-stalled approval leaves a dead member's storefront relaying client inquiries and a dead reviewer silently accruing never-to-be-scored assignments for an unbounded period.Settled: Split the fan-out: at deceased-pending (step 2), immediately suspend billing, take the RBPN listing down (or at minimum stop the relay), freeze reviewer assignments, and recompute conflicts. Reserve the family-approval gate strictly for publications: tree closure display, club news, magazine.
majorEmpty approval set: deceased-pending is a deadlock state with no exit
Because J04 step 3's approval set is exactly "household adults + reporter-if-one-degree" and escalation triggers only on explicit objection, a member who lived alone and is reported dead by anyone beyond one degree yields an empty approval set with no timeout, fallback, or override — deceased-pending becomes a permanent deadlock in which the tree never closes and no publication or seat-vacancy ever releases.Settled: Define a fallback chain (nearest tree-linked adult within N degrees, then membership secretary after a fixed waiting period with documented external evidence) and a maximum dwell time in deceased-pending, after which the secretary may release with a logged rationale.
majorRefund of a deceased member's pre-paid dues breaks gross posting and the club-dues agency liability
After the club share of nationally-collected dues has been remitted (which deceased-pending does not suspend), a Board-approved refund cannot be executed by step 5's 'reversing line only' mechanism: the rule-21 agency liability is already zero, so the batch must either drive the club's agency account negative with no defined claw-back, post unbalanced in violation of rule 22, or charge national's books through an account the spec never defines.Settled: Refund mechanics need their own spec: reverse revenue/liability net of non-recoverable processing fees (fee stays as a real expense); post-remittance club-dues refunds create an explicit receivable from the club (or deduct from the next remittance batch) rather than a negative liability; refunds against a deceased payer route to the estate/surviving household with a payout-failed state that holds funds in clearing, not a fire-and-forget reversing line.
majorA false or pending death report silently strips a living member of standing across a dues/voting boundary
Because J04 step 2 suspends billing and dunning on identity resolution alone — before any family approval — a false death claim filed before the voting-dues deadline silently prevents a living member's dues from posting, and when the family rejects the claim no rule defines restoration (retroactive dues, tolling), while rules 12 and 17 make the resulting exclusion from the frozen electorate and remittance-ledger standing check irreversible for the cycle.Settled: Deceased-pending should suspend CHARGES but toll deadlines: record the suspension window, and on rejection of the claim, restore auto-renew, extend any dues/voting deadline that fell inside the window, and back-date standing for certification purposes. Log which ruleset's deadline was applied per rule 18.

Worked example: the false-report attack now fails twice — no evidence artifact, and the reporter cannot approve her own claim.
