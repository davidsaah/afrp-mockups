The Convention, in full

v1.0 · 20 Aug 2026 · four dimensions · every moment paired: running it / living it

Sixty-eight of these since 1959, and it is still the hardest thing the federation does: a four-day meeting that is simultaneously a family reunion, a legislative session, a trade show, a youth camp and a small hotel business — run by volunteers who change every year. This deep dive takes it in four dimensions: the year-long timeline from bid to handoff, role by role, hour by hour on-site, and the money — where three different legal treatments of a dollar meet in one ballroom and the platform has to name which one applies, every time.

Dimension 1 · the year that produces four days

The timeline — bid to handoff

A convention is not planned in the months before it; it is planned in the two years before it. The site is chosen by the membership two years ahead, rotating through three regions — which means at any moment three conventions are alive at once: the one being run, the one being planned, and the one being bid for. The platform has to hold all three without letting them contaminate each other.

1The bidR43 · 10.1
Running it — a club that wants the convention

San Jose’s officers assemble a bid: candidate hotels, room-block pricing, air access, the local committee who would actually do the work, and a draft budget built on the federation’s own template so bids can be compared like with like.

The bid is a club action filed with the federation — it does not commit the club’s money until a contract is signed, and the platform says so on the form.

Living it — a member who will vote on it

She sees the competing bids side by side in the convention section: cities, dates, indicative prices, and what each committee is promising. Not a glossy pitch — the same fields for everyone.

The vote is hers because the site is chosen by the general membership two years ahead, not by the Board. The ballot is the ordinary ballot machinery.

2Site selection and the contractConvention Contract · R43
Running it — federation staff and the incoming host

The membership’s choice becomes a year record: “Convention 2028 · 70th · San Jose”, an object of its own with its own committee, budget line and contract dates. Nothing from a prior year carries into it without an explicit review-and-adopt — not prices, not store items, not comms lists.

The Convention Contract is signed between the federation and the host. It is the document that makes the money question answerable, so the platform stores it as the source, not as an attachment nobody reads.

Living it — the winning club

San Jose learns what it has actually taken on: named deadlines, a required accounting timeline, and the fact that convention money is never its own.

The platform states the split at signing rather than at settlement: anything paid through My AFRP is Federation money from the first dollar — the host is class attribution, not a bank. Only what the host takes off the platform — cash at a door, a local cheque — is held as an agent, and that money posts as a receivable owed to the federation by the contract’s settlement date.

Contract & finance

Open: the contract’s own renewal cycle. The 2009 Convention Contractual Agreement is the text in force; whether it is re-executed per convention or standing with per-year schedules is a Board question the platform currently flags rather than assumes.

3Committee formationR38 · R39
Running it — the convention chair

The committee forms on the standard committee rails: the roster IS the grant, scoped program:convention. Registration, program, banquet, youth, hospitality, sponsorship, and the desk each get a lead with a scope — not a shared login.

Grants are end-dated to the convention. The chair does not have to remember to remove anyone.

Living it — a member who volunteers

He submits the Committee Interest Form, is accepted onto the banquet subcommittee, and immediately can see exactly what that gives him: the banquet capacity and seating tool, the run-of-show, the vendor contacts — and not the registration list, not anyone’s giving, not the roster.

When the convention closes, his access ends on the date it said it would.

4The budget, and the first hard numberR38 · R23
Running it — the convention treasurer

The budget is adopted as the convention’s own line: room-block guarantees, food-and-beverage minimums, AV, the youth program, the banquet, printing. The two numbers that decide everything are the attrition clause (rooms the federation must fill or pay for) and the F&B minimum.

Registration pricing is then derived from those, not guessed — the platform shows the derivation.

Living it — the Board

The Board sees the same derivation and can ask the only question worth asking: at what registration count does this convention break even, and what happens at 80% of it?

A budget adopted by the Board is an allocation. Only a donor’s designation creates a restriction — the screen says which is which, because that confusion has cost nonprofits real money.

Board workspace
5Registration opensR19 · R26 · S1
Running it — the registration lead

Registration is built on the one event builder under the convention policy pack: membership is mandatory for all attendees, so a “non-member” rate bundles the membership it requires and creates it through the front door at checkout. Guest tickets are refused, naming the rule.

Banquet seats are capacity inventory, not a donation line. There is no oversell path to find.

Living it — a member registering a family

She registers herself, her husband, and their fourteen-year-old — the household model decides who she may register, not a shared password. The youth program has its own consent-holder step.

Her Detroit club membership is lapsed, so one rate reads differently than she expected, and the screen tells her exactly why and what would change it.

5.5The governance calendar running underneathR12 · 8.11 · 9.2
Federation staff and the committees

Parallel to the logistics, a second calendar with hard dates: the amendment and resolution filing deadline, the nominations window for national office, the delegate certification cutoff, the record date that freezes the electorate, and the credentials report to the Assembly.

These are the dates that decide whether the convention can lawfully do business, so they belong on the same timeline as the hotel deadlines rather than in a separate memo.

A member with something to file

She wants to put a by-law amendment before the Assembly. The platform tells her the filing deadline and what it must contain — before it passes, not after.

5.7Housing and the room blockbudget · contract
The chair, watching the attrition clause

Room-block pickup is tracked against the guarantee, because unfilled rooms are a bill the federation pays. The chair sees pickup against the clause with time to act.

A member booking

He books through the block link by the cutoff. Booking outside it is his right and costs the federation money, so the screen says so plainly rather than nagging.

Rooming money is the hotel’s, never the federation’s — it appears nowhere in the accounting except as the attrition exposure.

5.9The registration lifecycle after purchaseR26 · R31 · S3
The registration lead

A published refund deadline schedule; substitution rules (a registration may be transferred to another member by the payer, once, before the deadline — a ticket is possession, so the transfer is recorded rather than assumed); the walk-up path on site; and accessibility and dietary fields that only the people who must act on them can read.

Plans that change

She can cancel by a date she was told, hand her place to her sister before the deadline, or arrive and register at the desk. During the summer registration pause the screen says what is happening: payments queue as pending-capture, waitlist positions hold.

6Close-out and settlementR43 · S3 · 10.1.7
Running it — treasurer and staff together

Within the accounting deadline the host renders a full accounting: every dollar collected, every dollar spent, the surplus or deficit, and the settlement transfer. Snapshots — the roll, sales, attendance — archive read-only.

Unclaimed store pickups are held, then refunded through the reversal model. Nothing is quietly absorbed.

Living it — the host club

The host club finds out whether it is owed money or owes it — and, crucially, that the answer was computable all along, because the treatment of each dollar was named when it was collected rather than reconstructed afterwards.

7HandoffR43 · R37
Running it — outgoing chair to incoming chair

The next year’s record opens empty by default. What carries is deliberate and reviewed: the lessons file, the vendor performance notes, and — the one standing exception — program-book ad placements that missed this year’s book roll forward through the ads desk rather than vanishing.

Living it — next year’s committee

They inherit a checklist and a set of decisions with reasons attached, instead of a folder of last year’s files and a phone number for someone who is tired.

Journey J13

Worth deciding: sponsor relationships are the other thing hosts want carried forward. The design currently treats each year’s sponsorships as that year’s — a multi-year sponsor agreement would be an inter-entity-style agreement record, not a copied list. Flagged, not assumed.

The through-line. Every rail of the federation surfaces here once a year — camp reunion, scholarship awards on stage, the RBPN meetup, the heritage exhibit, the mission report to the floor. That is why the convention is its own element rather than a big event: it is the annual convening layer of the program rails.

The commonest planning failure in volunteer organisations is last year’s spreadsheet becoming this year’s plan by accident. Empty-by-default is the design’s answer, and review-and-adopt is the escape hatch.

Dimension 2 · seven people, seven surfaces

Role by role

The same four days look completely different depending on who you are. Each role below gets a different surface and a different scope — and the scope is the point: a badge-reprint agent does not need your giving history, and a delegate does not need the banquet seating chart.

1The convention chairR39
What they hold

The command centre: registration counts against capacity, room-block pickup against the attrition clause, banquet sales against the F&B minimum, desk queue length, and the exception list — what is overdue or blocked right now.

What they deliberately cannot see

Not the sealed ballots. Not individual giving. Not the credentialing determinations, which belong to the credentials chair by rule.

A chair who can see everything is a chair who can be lobbied about everything.

Command centre
2The convention treasurerR20 · R23 · S3
What they hold

Every dollar with its treatment named at the moment of collection: registration and Relief Fund as Federation property held by an agent, sponsorships by class, the store, the banquet. Daily close, gross fees, reversals as receivables.

What the design forces them to do

They cannot post an unclassified dollar. There is no “miscellaneous” bucket to sweep the difficult ones into — which is inconvenient for four days and decisive for the ninety after.

Daily close
3The credentials chairR12 · R17 · S4 · S6
What they hold

The frozen roll at the record date, delegate certifications verified against the remittance ledger rather than self-attestation, and the exception queue where a club feed and the ledger disagree.

What a refused delegate sees

“Detroit’s fourth delegate cannot be seated: the club’s August remittance is unposted — By-Law 8.11.” The rule, the date, and the fix. Never a shrug, never a list of who else was refused.

Credentialing
4A delegateS4 · R14
What the floor gives them

The current weight of their delegation shown live, the motion before the house, and a vote that freezes weights the moment voting opens — so a member extracting mid-vote cannot change the value of ballots already cast.

What they carry

They carry other people’s votes. The arithmetic is visible to the room in exact hundredths, because a certified result that fails to reconcile by a fraction of a vote is a result somebody litigates.

Live floor
5An ordinary attendeeR19 · R30 · R31
What the platform does for them

A rotating QR badge, a schedule that updates, stamped operational announcements, and one two-way door — the help desk — that is owned and clocked rather than being “ask whoever is at the table”.

Their four days

They came to see people. The measure of the platform is how little of it they have to touch: badge at the door, schedule in a pocket, and nothing that requires learning software during a reunion.

6A youth participant (and their consent-holder)R7 · R40 · S1
What the youth lead holds

The youth roster with health forms gating arrival, medication logs double-signed, incident closure requiring a parent notification, and pickup authority read from the household model — the camp rules, generalized to four days in a hotel.

What the parent controls

The parent set photo consent and pickup authority once, in the household, and it is honoured here without being re-asked. Their fourteen-year-old appears on no attendee-visible list at all.

Journey J07
7A vendor or sponsorR19 · R39
What sponsorship holds

Sponsorship is a money class with deliverables attached (book ad, signage, table). The ads desk carries the program-book deadline; the sponsor sees their own placements and nothing else.

The awkward case, handled

An exhibitor credential is not attendance and therefore does not touch R19: hall access only — no sessions, no banquet, no floor, no vote, and no line on any roll. A vendor’s representative who wants to attend the program registers normally, as a member, like everyone else.

That distinction keeps the rule absolute instead of carving an exception into it. Press and observer credentials work the same way.

Desk & storeAdvertise

Why it matters: a “vendor exception to membership” would put non-members on a credentialed list that the electorate is derived from. A non-attendee credential cannot inflate a roll, because it never creates a membership in the first place.

8The host club’s own officers — the role the money depends onConvention Contract · R23
Who actually holds the account

The host club president and treasurer are distinct from the convention chair and convention treasurer, and the platform records which individuals hold which. Where the host treasurer is the convention treasurer, that is a recorded fact, not an assumption.

The duty, named

Because the agency duty attaches to a named person and a named account. “The host holds it as an agent” means nothing until the record says who.

9Tellers, parliamentarian, press and observers9.2 · R39
Election committee and chair

Tellers are a scoped role for the counting hours only; the parliamentarian advises and records nothing. Press and observer credentials are non-attendee credentials with named access.

Who may be in the room

Business sessions have a defined observer status, so “can I sit at the back?” has an answer before someone has to improvise one at the door.

Dimension 3 · the four days themselves

Hour by hour on-site

On-site is where a design either survives or gets replaced by a clipboard. The test is not the happy path — it is 6:40pm at the banquet door with a queue, a scanner that will not scan, and a family who swear they paid.

1Wednesday, 2:00pm — the desk opensR19 · R31
The desk agent

Search by name, confirm the registration, print badges. The door list shows name and ticket type only — not giving, not consents, not household — because a desk screen is visible to everyone in the queue behind it.

The arriving family

Ninety seconds, four badges, and the youth wristband handled at the same table instead of a second queue somewhere else.

2Wednesday, 2:20pm — the wifi diesR22 · R43
The desk agent

The desk keeps working from the pre-loaded arrival snapshot: check-ins queue locally and reconcile when the connection returns, idempotently — the same queue-and-replay pattern the club ledgers use when a QuickBooks token dies.

Payments do not run offline. Money waits for the network; attendance does not.

The queue

Nobody in the queue notices. That is the entire specification.

QBO queue pattern

Design note: offline check-in means two devices can admit the same badge before they reconcile. The settled answer is that duplicate admission is detected at reconcile and reported, never silently merged — a badge scanned twice is a fact worth knowing, not an error worth hiding.

3Thursday, 9:00am — the floorR12 · S4
The chair and credentials

Quorum tested against the frozen roll; the mode for each motion assigned by the by-laws, not chosen by the chair; weights fixed when voting opens on each question.

The room

The room sees the arithmetic on the screen as it happens — Chicago’s 90.00 dividing across five delegates, re-dividing as members extract between motions, and totalling to exactly 90.00 every time.

Live floor
3.5Thursday, 10:30am — the electionR13 · R14 · 9.2
Tellers and the election committee

Ballots issue against the frozen roll; the tellers’ station is a scoped surface for the counting hours only. The mailed CPA ballots reconcile with the floor into one count — never “the electronic result plus the paper ones”.

Results are announced with the arithmetic shown, and a challenge window opens with a stated closing time.

A voter

She votes once, in whichever channel she was issued. Her receipt proves her ballot is in the box and proves nothing about its contents — which is what makes it safe to hold.

3.7Friday, 8:00am — the memorial serviceS2 · R30
The committee

The convention remembers members who died since the last one. The list compiles from family-approved life events only — never from the deceased-pending state, and never as a surprise to a family who has not yet approved publication.

Families

Names read aloud that the families expected to hear. No attendance is recorded.

4Thursday, 6:40pm — the banquet doorR26 · R31
The banquet lead

Seats are inventory: the count at the door is the count that was sold, and there is no oversell path to have created the problem. A scan failure falls back to name lookup on the same snapshot.

The family who swear they paid

They did pay — for three, not four. The desk can see the purchase, the tier, and the date, and can sell the fourth seat if one exists. If it does not, the refusal is capacity, stated plainly, not a judgement about them.

5Friday, 11:00am — the awardsS7 · R27
The scholarship committee

Winners are named on stage with per-instance consent. That consent screen now says what it must: this naming plus the published funnel report could, together, identify who applied and did not receive.

A recipient, and a non-recipient

The recipient chose to be named. The applicant who was not selected is protected by complementary suppression in the report — the red team found that exact subtraction attack and it is why the rule exists.

5.5Any hour — a medical emergency in the lobbyR40-pattern · R27
The committee

An incident involving an adult is logged and closed by the committee; an incident involving a minor closes only with a consent-holder notification, exactly as at camp. The desk can reach a youth participant’s consent-holder because the youth roster carries it — the ordinary door list does not, and does not need to.

An adult attendee, and a youth

6Saturday, 11:00pm — hospitalityR30
Hospitality leads

Suite schedules are operational notices to registrants. Nothing about hospitality rides marketing, and no attendance is recorded — there is no legitimate use for a list of who was in which suite at midnight.

Everyone else

The part of the convention that matters most to the most people is the part the platform touches least. That is deliberate.

7Sunday, 10:00am — teardownR43
The committee

Attendance, sales and roll snapshots freeze. Unclaimed store items go to a 30-day hold. The lessons file is written now, while it is still true, rather than in the exhausted week afterwards.

The record

Everything anyone will argue about in November was captured in the moment it happened, stamped with the ruleset that governed it.

Dimension 4 · the hard one

Money and the contract lifecycle

In one ballroom, on one evening, three different legal treatments of a dollar are in play at once. Getting this wrong is how volunteer organisations end up in disputes that outlast everyone involved. The platform’s job is to name the treatment at the moment of collection — not to reconstruct it at settlement.

1Registration and the Relief Fund — federation money from the first dollarR19 · R22 · R23
The convention treasurer

Every convention payment runs through My AFRP, so it is federation money at the moment of capture and posts at the federation’s daily close. The host city is a class code on it — attribution, not ownership, and never a bank account the close cannot reach.

One registration can carry three things at once: a membership (liability until remitted), a Relief Fund gift (donor-restricted), a banquet seat (inventory). They are three lines, not one deposit.

What that means in practice

Which removes the classic failure: convention money sitting in a club account, spent on a club expense, and reconciled optimistically in November.

The label travels with the dollar from the first checkout, and the accounting is a report of what the ledger already knows rather than a reconstruction.

2The exception: what the host takes off-platformConvention Contract · S3
The host treasurer

Cash at a door, a local cheque, a table sold at the hall — receipts that never touch My AFRP. This is where the Convention Contract’s agency treatment applies: the host holds federation money as an agent.

The agent duty, precisely scoped

It posts as a receivable owed by the host to the federation, with the contract’s settlement date as its due date. Not the club’s revenue for a while; not a mystery until spring. A receivable with a name and a deadline.

2The host club’s own hafli, during convention weekR21
The host club

A club event during convention week is club money — the club’s own revenue, collected nationally and held as a liability until remitted. A completely different treatment, five feet away, on the same night.

Why this is the dangerous one

This is where the seams tear. Two cash boxes, two treatments, one volunteer holding both. The design answer is that the event object carries the host field, and money routes by that field — never by which table it was taken at.

Event builder
3A club project fund passing through HQ11.1.5 · R25
The federation

Club project funds route through headquarters as a conduit: the money is neither federation revenue nor host revenue — it passes through with its restriction intact.

The third treatment

Three treatments, one weekend: owned by the federation, held by an agent; the club’s own; passing through. Every screen that shows a dollar says which one it is.

Multi-entity ledger
4Sponsorships and the program bookR37 · R23
The sponsorship lead

Sponsorship is federation revenue with deliverables attached — an exchange, not a gift, so the receipt says so and the deductible portion is stated. Where a package includes seats or a table, those seats are drawn from capacity inventory like any other, not conjured.

The ads desk carries the program book’s real deadline and a missed placement rolls forward rather than vanishing.

The sponsor

The sponsor sees what they bought, when it is due, and what was delivered — the most common reason a sponsor does not renew is not price, it is never finding out whether their ad ran.

5A refund after settlementS3
Both treasurers

A chargeback lands in October, after the 90-day accounting closed and the settlement transferred. Nothing is edited: reversing lines post, and because the money already crossed a boundary, the reversal creates a receivable — not a negative balance in a closed period.

The reversal model doing its job

The frozen accounting stays frozen, annotated “payment reversed post-settlement”. Anyone reading it later sees both facts: what was true at close, and what happened after.

Journey J02
6A deficit10.1.7
The Board

The convention loses money — attrition was missed, attendance was soft. The budget line shows it against the adopted budget, and the accounting deadline forces the conversation while the facts are fresh.

The host club

Who bears it is a governance question, not a software one — but the platform ensures it is asked with a full and timely accounting in hand rather than a year later with fragments.

Board decision: deficit responsibility between the federation and a host city is defined by the Convention Contract; the platform reads it rather than assuming. Where the contract is silent, the screen says so instead of guessing.

Why this dimension got its own section. Every other part of the convention can be recovered from if it goes wrong — a bad schedule is annoying, a slow desk is forgivable. Money handled under the wrong treatment produces disputes between a club and the federation that can last decades, and the people involved are neighbours and cousins. The platform’s contribution is narrow and specific: name the treatment when the dollar arrives, and never let a screen show a dollar without it.
Red-teamed before publication. Eighteen attacks across the five deep dives — offline oversell, vendor badges inflating the electorate, the treasurer voting his own audit, a post-settlement chargeback with no owner — all refuted. Sixteen consistency findings were folded in, and the largest changed this document: convention money paid through My AFRP is federation money at the first dollar, with the Convention Contract’s agency treatment scoped precisely to off-platform receipts. An earlier draft had the host holding all of it as an agent, which contradicted the rule that every convention payment runs through the platform.

If a volunteer can answer “whose money is this?” for every dollar they touch during those four days, the convention will settle cleanly. That sentence is the whole design brief for this dimension.
