Scholarship applications and review — median ranking, spread, and a recusal that is not an empty review

Ranking uses the median, not the mean, so one harsh or generous reviewer cannot move an outcome. Spread is surfaced, not smoothed: two reviewers 57 points apart on the same application is a signal the rubric was read differently, and averaging it hides exactly the case the committee should discuss. Ties are left tied and flagged — Rana Salameh and Basim Yacoub both sit at 75.0 and the system will not separate them. And the third row down carries the distinction this whole screen exists for: Ghassan Haddad’s review of Layla Khoury is recused, which is a different state from not scored, with a different count, forever. An audit of a contested award turns on that difference.

Ties, left tied

Rana Salameh and Basim Yacoub, both 75.0.

The engine returns them tied and stops. Their means are identical too, at 75.7. Breaking the tie is the committee’s job, in a minuted discussion, on criteria they can state. Software choosing on an alphabetical sort or a hidden tie-break is how a family finds out a decision was arbitrary.

In this cycle it resolves itself: their fields and clubs qualify them for different named awards, so the most-constrained-first matching on frame 17 separates them without anyone having to rank one above the other.

Conflict detection — the family tree doing operational work

AFRP is a federation of families descended from one ancestor. A committee member being related to an applicant is normal, not scandalous — scoring your own great-niece without saying so is the problem. So conflicts are detected, not merely declared: the tree is walked at assignment, and a relationship within three degrees blocks the review outright. Beyond three degrees it is disclosed on the record and not blocked, because a six-degree bar in this community would leave no committee at all. This is the one screen where the genealogy archive stops being ceremonial. It is the only system that knows a reviewer is an applicant’s grand-uncle, and that is precisely the relationship self-declaration misses: near enough to matter, far enough to feel deniable.

Why not six degrees

The arithmetic of a small community.

113 reviewer–applicant pairsat each threshold
within 3° — 3 pairs4–6° — 7 pairsbeyond — 103
BarBlockedEffect on the committee
3 degrees3Workable. Every application keeps at least two reviewers
4 degrees6Two applications drop to a single reviewer
6 degrees10No committee at all. Everyone descends from one ancestor

The bar is not a moral position, it is a threshold chosen so the program can run. Disclosure carries everything above it, which is why the disclosure register is published to the board rather than kept as an internal note.

What the tree is not used for

Boundaries on an operational archive.

Provisional recordsNever feed a conflict decision, and never reach print. Only canonical, moderated records are walked.
Legacy statusWhether an applicant’s parent was a scholar is modelled as context only, deliberately, until the board says otherwise.
Contact detailsA tree path never reveals contact information — the rule that governs alumni tracing on frame 18 applies here too.

What this screen decides

  • Whether an award survives being questioned by a family that did not receive one. The path, the degree and the rule are attached to the review forever.
  • Whether the genealogy archive earns its maintenance cost. This is the query no commercial CRM can run, because it needs a tree AFRP has been keeping since 1952.

Rules made visible

  • DetectedWalked at assignment, before the reviewer sees the application. Not a checkbox on a form.
  • Disclosed above 3Beyond three degrees the relationship is recorded and the review proceeds. Blocking it would empty the committee.
  • Canonical onlyProvisional tree records are never walked. An unmoderated submission cannot disqualify a reviewer.

Detail notes

  • The household rule fires before the tree is walked, because two people at one address is a conflict whatever the genealogy says.
  • The tree does two operational jobs in this program: this one, and verifying descent for eligibility — the program is open to descendants, not to members.
  • Is three degrees the right bar? It is a default, not a rule, and it is one of the open questions for the committee rather than for the software.

Budget, encumbrance and named awards — the arithmetic that quietly breaks programs

A four-year award made in 2026 commits money in 2027, 2028 and 2029. If the committee counts only this year’s new awards against this year’s budget it will over-commit, and the failure appears two years later as a scholar whose funding evaporates mid-degree — the worst outcome this program can produce, and it is arithmetic rather than fundraising. So AVAILABLE is the only number the committee is shown: $60,000 less $16,000 already encumbered for continuing scholars less $29,000 committed to new awards equals $15,000. Below it, the endowment capacity check, which found in August what would otherwise have been found after an offer letter: Zarou Engineering cannot fund the award that bears its name.

Two disbursements are flagged and cannot pay. One scholar is a non-resident alien on an F-1 visa with US-source taxable income; one is studying at Birzeit, where the grant is likely foreign-source and outside US withholding entirely. The software records facts and flags cases; it never decides. A named adviser clears each one, and the clearance is recorded against the disbursement.

Refusing with a reason matters: “the payment didn’t go through” with no explanation is how a scholar misses a tuition deadline.

What this screen decides

  • How many new scholars can be funded without stranding an existing one two years from now. That is $15,000, and it is the only figure the committee should be arguing over.
  • Whether the Zarou family gets a phone call this month or an apology next spring.

Rules made visible

  • EncumbranceMulti-year awards commit future cycles at the moment of offer, not at the moment of payment.
  • Most constrained firstNamed awards are filled before the general pool, so restricted money is spent as the donor intended.
  • Reported with a reasonEvery unfilled award and unmatched scholar says whether the constraint was funding, criteria or competition.
  • Flagged cannot payA tax case waits for a named adviser. The system never computes tax.

Detail notes

  • The Salameh Award was established by a 1994 recipient. It is shown with zero spendable rather than hidden, because a scholarship funded by a former scholar is the whole thesis of the alumni work on frame 18.
  • Endowment values use a trailing three-year average so awards do not lurch with the market — but that is platform practice, not either document’s text: nothing states whether “all Assets” are valued on 31 May, 1 June, or an average, and the difference materially changes every award budget.
  • The 1% administration distribution to AFRP is a real related-party transaction — expense in ARFECF, revenue in AFRP, disclosed in both sets of books.
  • Whether the committee decides within an envelope or recommends to the board is still open. Modelled here as recommend-and-approve.

ARFHSN — Human Services Network

The third institution: a 501(c)(3) with no membership of its own, a 14-seat Board of Trustees elected by the AFRP Board, and by-laws amendable by 60% of that Board. Its programs ride the shared programme rails; its governance is an interlock with AFRP’s, not a copy of it. Everything here is roadmap — badged accordingly.

Governing text: ARFHSN By-Laws 2015/2017 · ruleset arfhsn-2017 · trustee roster outstanding
