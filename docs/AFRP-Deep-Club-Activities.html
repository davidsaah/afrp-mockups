A club’s year

v1.0 · 20 Aug 2026 · the least-specified surface, specified · every moment paired

This is the surface the platform has said least about, and it is the one most members actually experience. A club is not a database of members; it is a rhythm — a meeting every month, a hafli season, the kids’ activities, the holidays, the funerals, the fundraiser, and the delegation that drives to convention. Software that does not fit that rhythm gets abandoned for a phone tree, and deservedly. This deep dive walks the year and marks, at each point, the one thing the platform must not get wrong.

The shape of a club year

The rhythm — and what runs it

Everything below runs on the one event builder under a club policy pack. There is no separate “club activity” system, because a club that has to learn two tools will use neither. What changes between a general meeting and a funeral is not the machinery — it is the visibility, the consent class, and whether attendance is recorded at all.

1The three visibility settings, chosen onceEVENTS · R28
The officer creating an activity

OPEN, CLUB-ONLY and INVITED are the club pack’s plain-language labels for the event builder’s existing visibility field — the same field, not a second vocabulary — and they compose with the guests-allowed toggle and the member-rate scope (host-club / any-club / national).

Whatever it is set to is shown on every surface the activity appears on. Changing it later is an edit under the event-manage grant, effective-dated and logged: people already registered keep their place, so a narrowing never becomes a public un-inviting.

What a member sees

He never has to guess whether he is welcome. An activity that is club-only says so where he finds it, not when he arrives.

2What is recorded, and what is not6.3.1 · R27
The default

Attendance at club activities is never a governance input — no exceptions, none. The club check-in surface is incapable of writing a governance-attendance record.

By-Law 6.3.1 ties a Past President’s vote to attendance at conventions and Mid-Year meetings — and only those two instruments write that record. A club dinner is not either of them, however the same QR code happens to work at both doors.

Why it matters

Coming to dinner is coming to dinner. The federation keeps no participation record that will matter to her later.

The distinction, enforced not assumed: convention and Mid-Year check-ins are retained because a bylaw requires them; club activity attendance is retained only as long as the activity needs it, and the check-in screen says which kind of door you are standing at.

Most of the year

Meetings, haflis, youth and holidays

The ordinary occasions — which are the point of belonging to a club at all.

1The monthly general meetingEVENTS · club rules
The club secretary

Recurring, club-only, free, no ticket. Agenda posted; minutes taken under the club’s own rules and kept in the club’s own record — the federation reads them only if the club shares them.

A member deciding whether to go

A calendar entry, a reminder on the operational class, and an agenda she can read beforehand — which is the difference between attending and just being told afterwards.

Club events
2The hafli — the big oneEVENTS · R21 · R26
The dinner-dance committee

Ticketed, capacity-controlled, table seating, a band, a hall deposit. Built on the event builder: tiers (adult / child / under-4 free), the hall’s capacity as real inventory, and money routed by the host field — this is the club’s own revenue, held as a liability until remitted.

A family of five

He buys five seats in one checkout from the household, gets one QR per person, and sits with the people he asked to sit with.

2.5The neighbours who are not membersEVENTS · R31
The organiser

A dinner-dance is largely spouses, neighbours and cousins-by-marriage, so the hafli carries a non-member guest tier. A guest ticket is a seat and never a membership: it creates a minimal guest-contact record — operational notices only, photo consent off by default, purged when the event archives.

A guest

She comes as someone’s guest, is not signed up for anything, and does not receive a newsletter afterwards because she once bought a chicken dinner.

2.7Walk-ups at the door, and the cash boxR26 · R21
The door

Walk-ups draw against remaining inventory — the door cannot sell a seat the hall does not have, which is the whole reason capacity is inventory rather than a target.

A door sale taken through the builder is platform money. A door sale taken in cash is not, and the design says so rather than pretending the cash box does not exist.

Where that money goes

The attendance snapshot at close is the sum of both channels, so the club’s record of the night is complete even though the money took two paths.

3Cross-listing to the neighboursEVENTS · R30
The organiser

San Jose lists San Francisco’s hafli: it appears on San Jose’s calendar and San Jose sends one promotion, on the marketing class. The listing never touches San Francisco’s money, capacity or roster, and San Jose sees only end-of-day counts.

A member of another club

She hears about the neighbouring club’s night from her own club, once — not four times from four directions, and never dressed up as an operational notice.

4Youth activitiesR7 · R40 · R4
The youth chair

Minors register through a consent-holder; the camp safeguarding pattern applies in miniature — pickup authority from the household, photo consent honoured, no minor on any attendee-visible list.

A parent

She authorises once, in the household, and it holds across the club’s activities, camp and the convention youth program. She is not re-consenting every season.

5Holidays — Easter, Eid, ChristmasEVENTS · R30
The club

Recurring occasions with real capacity and a volunteer roster. Nothing special technically — which is the point: the machinery should disappear behind the occasion.

Everyone

The club he grew up in still gathers at the same time of year, and finding out about it takes one notice rather than knowing the right person.

The part that must be handled with care

Condolences, funerals and the news nobody wants to be first with

Every club’s year includes deaths. This is the surface where getting the design wrong causes real harm to real families, and where the temptation to automate is strongest and most dangerous. The rule that governs everything here: the platform never announces a death.

1The club learns of a deathS2
The club president

The club does not learn it from the platform first. A death enters as a claim, moves to verified-pending once the desk has an evidence artifact, and publications wait for family approval — club news included.

What fires at verified-pending are operational effects only: billing suspends, a professional listing comes down, assignments freeze. None of those is an announcement.

The family

They tell people themselves, in the order they choose. Nobody learns from a newsletter that a cousin has died.

Journey J04Life events
2The condolence gatheringEVENTS · R30 · S2
The club, once the family has approved

A condolence gathering is an event with rules of its own. Ordinary event notices reach registrants on the operational class and everyone else on marketing — but grief may never ride marketing, and a condolence notice must reach the club before anyone has registered. So the club pack carries a bereavement notice type inside the operational class: scoped to the deceased’s club roster, sendable only after the family releases the life event, and refused before that with the rule named.

The attendee list is never published; there is no ticket, no capacity game, no promotion; and the builder refuses cross-listing on a condolence event.

It is created by the club at the family’s request, not automatically on approval.

Those who come

People come. No one is counted, no one is listed, and nobody receives a follow-up asking how their experience was.

Deliberately absent: there is no “condolences” feature — no wall, no reactions, no attendance badge. The design’s contribution to a funeral is to carry an accurate notice to the right people and then get out of the way.

3In Memoriam, laterR36 · S2
The correspondent

The magazine’s In Memoriam section compiles from approved life events and queues for the next copy freeze after approval. Never the one before.

The family, months on

They see it when they expected to see it, worded as they approved it. A correction afterwards is a new version, never a quiet edit.

Money and volunteer effort

Fundraisers, sign-up sheets and the club’s own books

A club runs on two currencies — small amounts of money and large amounts of unpaid effort — and the platform must be careful with both.

1The fundraiserR21 · R23 · 11.1.5
The treasurer

Three treatments to keep straight, and the platform names each: the club’s own revenue (hafli tickets); a club project fund routed through headquarters as a conduit; and a gift to a federation program that happens to be collected at a club event.

A donor at the club

Her receipt says which entity received her gift and what it was designated for — because deductibility follows the entity, not the room.

Remittance
2The sign-up sheetPATHWAYS · R27
The hospitality chair

Volunteer asks ride the operational class to the activity’s registrants and the club’s volunteer roster — not the program-invitation class, which is reserved for milestone handoffs along the program rails. A member who mutes recruitment must not thereby stop hearing their own club ask for help with the chairs.

A decline is recorded nowhere a committee can hold against anyone. What the chair sees is the aggregate — how many slots are still open — which is the thing she actually needs.

A member who cannot help this time

He says no to the November setup crew and it costs him nothing in December. Volunteer effort is not a score.

The tension, stated: a chair with nobody signed up needs to know that, and does — as a count. What she cannot get is a list of who declined, because that list would change how people answer.

3The club’s own booksR42
The treasurer

The line is drawn by collection channel, not by occasion — and getting this backwards is how a club double-counts its own hafli.

Sold through the builder (card, My AFRP): federation-collected, held as a liability for the club, and it appears on the remittance statement. Taken off-platform (cash at the door, raffle tickets, the bar): the club’s own books, which the platform never edits and never claims to have seen.

The federation

The remittance statement says explicitly that it covers only what the federation collected, so a treasurer reconciling it never mistakes it for a complete picture of the evening.

Once a year

The delegation that goes to convention

The club’s year has one moment where it stops being local: choosing who carries its votes.

1Choosing delegatesR17 · S4
The club

The club names its delegates under its own rules. Standing is then verified by the federation against the remittance ledger — not self-attestation — and an unposted remittance surfaces as a credentialing gate, worded exactly as it is on the finance screen.

A member who wants to go

She learns she is certified — or exactly why not, with the rule and the date, in time to fix it.

Credentialing
2On the floorS4
The delegate

Her delegation’s weight is the club’s certified count divided by delegates sent, fixed the moment voting opens on each question.

The members back home

Any certified member present may extract their own vote and cast it themselves — the club’s total stays exactly what it was, and the arithmetic is on the screen.

Live floorThe Convention deep dive
The test for this whole surface. If a club officer with a full-time job and a family can run the year from a phone in the ten minutes between things, the design works. If any occasion requires learning something new, it will be run on WhatsApp instead — and the federation will be the last to know what happened.
Red-teamed before publication. The sweep corrected three things here: a condolence notice had no lawful channel under the existing consent classes, so the club pack now carries a bereavement notice type inside the operational class, released only by the family; volunteer sign-ups moved off the program-invitation class (reserved for milestone handoffs) so that muting recruitment never silences your own club; and club money is now split by collection channel, not occasion — builder-sold tickets are federation-collected and appear on the remittance statement, only off-platform cash is the club’s own book.

How to read this. Every moment is shown twice — the person running it on the left, the person living it on the right — because the gap between those two views is where this design has failed under attack before. Amber notes mark a gap or an open decision rather than a settled answer.
