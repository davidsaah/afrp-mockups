The programs, one at a time

v1.0 · 20 Aug 2026 · six sections · one shared shape · every moment paired

Nineteen programs, from a summer camp to a surgical mission to a magazine that has published since 1952. The claim this document tests is that they all run on one shape — charter, committee, budget, calendar, enrollment, metrics — and that only the activity differs, never the rails. Each of the six sections gets a representative program walked end to end in both views: the chair running it, and the member living it. Where a program strains the shared shape, the strain is marked rather than smoothed over.

The claim

One shape, six sections

Every program the federation runs — however different it looks from the outside — moves through the same six stations. A chair who has run one can run another. A member who has been through one knows what the next will ask of them. That predictability is not blandness; it is what lets volunteers hand things over without losing them.

1Charter — what this program is forR38
The chair’s shape

Every program carries a mission, a committee drawn from the membership, a budget, a list of activities, and defined metrics of success. Not a slogan — the five fields the Board actually reviews.

A program without metrics cannot answer the only question that matters at budget time, so the platform will not let one exist without them.

The member’s shape

She can read what a program is trying to do before deciding whether to give it a year of her life. The mission is the same text the Board reviews, not a recruitment version of it.

2Committee — the roster is the grantR38 · R39
Forming it

The Committee Interest Form feeds an intake queue; acceptance onto the roster is the grant (person, role, program:x). There is no separate permissions step to forget.

Joining it

He sees exactly what the seat gives him — roster, minutes, budget line, pipeline counts — and what it does not: applicant consents, payment methods, another program’s people.

3Budget — allocation, not restrictionR38 · S6
Holding the line

The program’s line is an allocation the Board made. Only a donor’s designation creates a restriction. The screen says which is which, in those words, because the difference is legal.

Reading it honestly

A donor who gave “for camp” sees their gift stay for camp. A committee that was allocated funds sees that the Board can reallocate them — and is not surprised in March.

4Calendar — activities are eventsEVENTS · R19
No new rails

A program’s activities run on the one event builder every club uses, under the program’s own policy pack. A lecture, a mission briefing, a cohort weekend — same six steps.

One familiar flow

Registering for a program event feels identical to registering for a club dinner, because it is the same machinery. Nothing new to learn per program.

5Enrollment — invitation or self-servePATHWAYS · S7
Filling a cohort

Programs recruit two ways: milestone invitations along the rails (a camper ages out, a scholar graduates) and open self-serve entry. Both land as dated enrollment records.

Being asked

An invitation is an ask, never an enrolment. Declining is undated-neutral — no committee ever sees a “declined” flag, so saying no this year costs nothing next year.

7The annual report — and the program lifecycleR38 · S7
Closing the year

The shape ends where the Board picks it up: an annual report — activities delivered, metrics against target, budget consumed — and next year’s budget request, submitted together so the Board judges the ask against the year.

The report carries aggregates only: a named-award recipient and their need data never travel to a Board table that includes the reviewer the three-degree rule excluded from that very file.

Chartering and retiring

A new program is chartered by Board motion — a proposal in the registry with its five fields complete, adopted with an effective date. Retiring one is the same motion in reverse: the HS Senior Award sits in the registry as retired, its history intact and readable, its alumni still traceable. Programs do not disappear; they change state.

6Metrics — aggregates that survive arithmeticR38 · S7
Reporting up

The committee sees its own cohort counts, retention, placement, growth — aggregated, with complementary suppression, published as frozen periodic snapshots.

Being counted, safely

She appears in a number, never in a dashboard. Her own end-to-end story is visible to her, and sharing it is a per-instance consent she can decline.

Journey J16

Where the shape strains: a mission program needs credentialing and insurance riders; a publication needs an editorial calendar and a copy freeze; a camp needs health forms and safeguarding. These are additional required stations, not different rails — each section below marks its own.

Section · Camp

Camp Ramallah — the fullest operating system in the federation

Two residential weeks each August, ages 13–17. Camp is where the platform stops being a database and becomes an operations system: a health form is a gate, a medication is a double signature, and an incident is not closed until a parent has been told.

1January — applications openR40 · R4
The camp committee

Applications open under the household model with sibling-of-camper priority. Capacity is real (64 places against ~92 applications in the demo year) and the waitlist holds position rather than quietly dropping people.

A parent

She applies for her fourteen-year-old from the household screen — she may register her own child because the household says so, not because she knows a password.

CampJourney J07
2June — the health form gateR40
The camp nurse

No form, no check-in. Allergy plans flag the kitchen automatically; the medication list is built before anyone arrives rather than at a folding table on the first morning.

The parent

She sets the tree-nut allergy once. It reaches the kitchen, the nurse and the counsellor without her having to tell three people and hope.

3June — counsellor screeningR40 · S6
The committee

Screening is a required pipeline step, not a norm — a counsellor who has not cleared it cannot be assigned. Conflicts within three degrees of an applicant family block assignment.

An applicant counsellor

A blocked assignment reads “recused · rule 41”. It never says which relative triggered it, because the refusal must not leak the family tree.

4August — the week itselfR40 · R27
Counsellors and the nurse

Stamped daily updates. Medications double-signed, with a missed window escalating. The scraped knee is an incident that closes with a 14:20 parent notification — closure requires the telling.

The camper, and the parent at home

The parent gets the notification without having to ask. The camper appears in group photos only, never tagged, because that is what her consent said.

5The Thursday early pickupR6 · R40
The gate

Pickup authority reads the household and delegation model; changes happen on the household screen, and ID is checked at the gate against it.

The named adult

One named adult, authorised in advance, checked against the record, logged. The uncle who was not named does not get her, however well he is known.

6September — the handoffR43 · PATHWAYS
Closing the season

The roster snapshot is retained; photos publish per each camper’s consent; the metrics go to the Board with the budget.

The next rail

The seventeen-year-olds ageing out receive the HS Senior Award invitation — the first handoff on the youth rail, an ask rather than an enrolment.

Section · Educational

The Scholarship — the strongest spec in the platform

Six independent attacks in the first red-team round; none survived. The scholarship is the program whose rules were hardest to get right and are now the most load-bearing: conflict detection reads a family tree, ranking refuses to average, and a budget cannot promise what an endowment cannot fund.

1The applicationR41
The committee

Transcripts, need documentation, tree-linked lineage or a sponsorship path, and a 3.0 floor. Applications are program data, visible to this committee for this purpose only.

A 21-year-old applicant

He applies once. His lineage claim, if provisional, does not block him — membership and eligibility never wait on genealogy.

ApplicationsJourney J06
2ConflictsR41 · S6
The rubric

A reviewer within three degrees on the tree is blocked from that file. Recusal is not the same as unscored — the file is scored by the others and the recusal is recorded.

The reviewer who is family

His great-uncle on the committee simply never sees his file. The block reads “recused · rule 41” to everyone else, and names no relationship.

Conflicts
3RankingR41
Deciding

Median ranking, never mean. Wide spreads are flagged for conversation rather than averaged away; ties stay tied and go to the committee.

Being decided

Two reviewers 57 points apart is a discussion about him, not an arithmetic accident that buries him.

4The moneyR41 · R43
Encumbrance

Awards encumber the budget, with endowment capacity as a hard input — one number, from the spending-policy calculator, frozen at budget adoption. Named awards match most-constrained-first.

The award

An award that is offered is an award that is funded. Disbursement is gated on enrollment verification each term, and a miss pauses with a named reason rather than silently stopping.

5And then he is a memberR9 · PATHWAYS
What the program creates

A scholarship recipient gets a free student membership: a real national record at $0, standard consent defaults, no club auto-add.

What he receives

He is now a member in his own right at nineteen — which is how the youth rail continues to Project Hope and, years later, to the professional network.

Still open: the scholarship’s legal home — Ramallah Foundation or ARFECF — and the Endowed Fund’s entity. The platform posts to the configured fund class and says which, rather than guessing.

Section · Medical

The Medical Mission — where the platform stops at the border

Hosted by ARFHSN, a separate 501(c)(3). Volunteers travel, supplies ship, surgeries happen — and the platform deliberately holds almost none of it, because the moment a patient becomes a record in a membership system, something has gone badly wrong.

1Recruiting the teamR38 · R40
The mission lead

Volunteers enroll on the standard rails — and then the camp-grade safeguarding stations apply: licensure and credential verification gates the manifest. Unverified means not travelling.

A surgeon volunteering

She uploads credentials once; the expiry is tracked, and she is told before it lapses rather than at an airport.

Mission operations
2LogisticsR25 · entity
Shipping

Manifests, customs documents, and a signed handoff at every transfer — the chain of custody is the audit.

The in-country partner

The partner signs for what arrives. Their systems hold the clinical work; ours hold the shipment.

3The hard lineS7 · entity boundary
What the platform holds

Aggregate counts only — patients seen, procedures, villages — under complementary suppression so a small village cannot be identified by subtraction.

What it must never hold

No patient name ever crosses the entity boundary into AFRP systems. Not for reporting, not for fundraising, not for a moving story in the magazine.

4The moneyR20 · R25 · S3
The conduit

MED and W2W are restricted classes on ARFHSN’s books. Gross fees; a chargeback after disbursement becomes a program receivable rather than driving a restricted class negative.

A donor

Her $500 to Women to Women is receipted by the right entity, and the receipt says which one — because deductibility follows the entity, not the appeal.

Section · Cultural

Hathihe Ramallah and the Preservation Project — the programs that hold the memory

A magazine published since 1952 and an archive begun in 2020, plus the family tree they both feed. These are the programs where the platform’s rules about consent and correction stop being abstract: print is forever, and an archive outlives the person it describes.

1The issue takes shapeR36
The editor

Sections are auto-compiled from live data — life events, event listings. There is no manual entry path into an auto section, which is what stops the magazine and the record diverging.

A correspondent

Her job is the data, not the copy: enter the wedding properly and it appears correctly. A wedding she enters on someone’s behalf still needs both named people’s approvals.

Magazine opsJourney J09
2A member disputes an entryS5
The freeze

A dispute filed before the copy freeze blocks that entry — not the issue. It prints as held, not as wrong.

The member

Her correction is neither lost nor rushed into print half-verified. After the freeze, a correction is a new version — never an edit to an issue already mailed.

3In MemoriamS2 · R34
The gate

A death is held for family approval everywhere, magazine included, and queues for the next freeze after approval — never the one before it.

The family

They learn of it from a person, not from a magazine. That is a design decision, and it is the one the family remembers.

4The archiveS5 · R27
The Preservation Project

The archive ingests only consent-classed material. Print consent is its own switch and the archive honours it.

A donor of papers

What he donates is described the way he consented to, and a retraction is a new entry rather than a quiet deletion — the record of the correction is part of the record.

Preservation
5The tree underneathR32
The steward

Thirty thousand people, eight generations. Living individuals are restricted; three-generation privacy blanks are preserved deliberately; provisional records are marked unverified rather than quietly promoted.

A living person on it

Her presence on the tree is not a publication. The blank next to her mother’s name is not missing data — it is a boundary someone asked for.

Section · Leadership

Leadership Ramallah, the Day of Action, and Congressional Outreach

The advocacy rail: year-round relationships, one annual summit, and a leadership cohort. These programs have the least software and the most consequence, and the design’s contribution is mostly restraint — plus one thing it must get right, which is what a program may say about its members in public.

1The cohort formsR38 · PATHWAYS
Leadership Ramallah

A cohort is an enrollment set with a start and an end — the same dated records everything else uses, so alumni are knowable years later without a separate list.

A 24-year-old applying

She applies, is accepted, and years afterwards can still see her own cohort year on her profile — and choose whether it shows on her professional listing.

Leadership
2The Day of ActionR30 · EVENTS
The organiser

The summit runs on the event builder: travel logistics, briefing materials, meeting schedules. Logistics are operational; recruitment is not — the ask rides the program-invitation class.

A first-time participant

He said yes to an invitation, and what arrives afterwards is logistics, not more asking. If he had said no, nothing about the decline would follow him.

3Speaking for peopleR27 · R39
Congressional Outreach

Relationship records — offices, staffers, meeting notes — are program-scoped. Advocacy positions are the federation’s, adopted through governance, not a committee’s improvisation.

Every member

No member is publicly named as endorsing anything by virtue of membership. Individual participation is a consent, per instance, and the platform has no mechanism to imply otherwise.

Deliberate restraint: the advocacy rail is the least instrumented on purpose. Meeting notes and relationship maps are exactly the data a federation would most regret leaking, so the design keeps them scoped tight and resists building a CRM inside the CRM.

How to read this. Every moment is shown twice — the person running it on the left, the person living it on the right — because the gap between those two views is where this design has failed under attack before. Amber notes mark a gap or an open decision rather than a settled answer. People and money figures are fictional; programs, clubs, committees and by-law citations are real and sourced (afrp.org and the governing texts).
