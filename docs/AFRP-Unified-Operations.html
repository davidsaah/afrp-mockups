Unified Operations — Twenty-One Staff Screens

The whole staff-facing platform in one document. Not four applications: one data model plus a role-scoped policy layer, seen from four angles. A grant is (person, role, scope) where scope is federation, club or program — so a Detroit treasurer sees Detroit and stops, and a scholarship chair reaches their program across all 18 clubs and stops there. Access is role-based, never org hierarchy, and every frame below shows the acting grant in its sidebar. Built on AFRP Design System v2, inlined verbatim; each frame is a picture of a laptop screen, annotated above and dissected below.

Decisions current as of August 2026: voting will be conducted electronically — By-Law 9.2’s envelopes and postmarks are replaced, the receipt proves inclusion, never content, and identity resolution becomes an election-integrity control; the dues ladder is a versioned schedule (dues-2026.1 — Student $25 · Individual $90 · Family $150 · Manar $500 · Patron $1,000, all figures provisional); and the bylaws are being revised, with every governing rule held as versioned ruleset data (bylaws-2012.2 in force) — so this document’s governance frames now run By-Law 9.1.3’s weighted delegation, not the apportionment first drafted.

Why a blocking rule and not a heavy penalty. With a penalty, enough corroborating signal eventually outvotes the conflict — and in a community where cousins share names and addresses, that signal is abundant. Convention governance — weighted delegation, credentialing, quorum

In a contested convention a credentials committee has to explain why a delegate’s vote weighs what it weighs, not merely state it. By-Law 9.1.3 has no divisor, no rounding rule and no floor: each delegate independently casts the club’s certified count divided by the delegates it sent, and any certified member present may extract their own vote, re-dividing the pool. So the table shows the division in a column, exact to the hundredth, and the extraction panel shows the weight moving — Chicago’s 18.00 becoming 15.60 as twelve members walk up. Above it all sits the property everything else depends on: the certified roll was frozen at the record date and is never recomputed. Twenty-five people joined after 16 October and the house did not move.

Contents

What is real and what is not. The rules on these twenty-one screens are real design commitments, and most of them are running code. Role-scoped access where a grant is (person, role, scope), scope containment is directional, club and program are siblings, and five permissions are never inherited. Dual membership with two independent lapse states and no isInGoodStanding(person) function anywhere. Ballot eligibility frozen at a record date and never recomputed; weighted delegate votes in exact decimal under By-Law 9.1.3, with member extraction re-dividing the pool at question open; participation and sealed votes in tables that are never joined; a receipt that proves inclusion, never content, and cannot be reissued; ties returned undecided and then drawn from a bag (9.2.4); abstentions reported always and counted per motion type. Journal batches idempotent on (date, fund, kind), fees booked gross in three lines, and club dues credited to Due to Local Clubs as a liability under the conservative ASC 958 treatment. Club ledger modes of qbo, platform and none, with dead connections expected and never blocking national’s close. Scholarship conflicts detected from the family tree at three degrees and disclosed beyond it, a recusal that is a distinct state from an unscored review, encumbrance of future cycles, endowment capacity checked before an offer, and most-constrained-first matching. Magazine sections generated at the copy freeze with no manual entry path, deaths held for family approval, and silence that still publishes.

The figures. The weighted delegation run (Chicago 90 certified sending 5 delegates at 18.00 votes each, Detroit 26 sending 5 at 5.20, a house of 373.00 votes across 27 delegates from eleven clubs; twelve Chicago extractions re-dividing 18.00 to 15.60 under the 9.1.4 cap), the President’s weighted ballot (181.00 at 52.31%, 165.00 at 47.69%, 27.00 abstentions excluded, quorum met, decided), the daily close of 16 January 2026 (1,025.00, 1,308.00, 148.00 and 1,978.00 totalling 4,459.00, balanced), the August 2026 remittance run (3,360.00 payable with 40.00 carried) and the 2026–27 scholarship cycle (60,000 budget, 16,000 encumbered, 29,000 committed, 15,000 available; Haddad Memorial spendable 10,400 funding 2 of 2, Zarou Engineering spendable 3,600 funding 0 of 1) are taken verbatim from the working engines against seeded data. Everything around them — people, clubs, giving totals, rosters, orders and page counts — is fictional, and internally consistent across all twenty-one frames. The governance run covers eleven clubs because that is what the seed contains; the same engine runs unchanged over all 26 once AFRP’s bylaws are lodged as parameters.

What is still open, and it changes the shape of the work. The bylaws have now been read against the design, and the governance frames follow them: weighted delegation (9.1.3), a flat quorum of 25 paid members (10.1.5), ties drawn from a bag (9.2.4), and electronic voting replacing 9.2’s mailed procedure. Still genuinely open, and asked of the revision: Assembly abstentions in or out of the denominator, per motion type; postmark versus receipt for the 28 February dues test (5.3 vs 8.15.5); whether complimentary memberships vote (8.15.4); and the 4% floor versus 5% cap on scholarship spending. The agency-versus-revenue answer from AFRP’s CPA gates the entire remittance build and moves the 990 materially. How many of the 18 clubs keep books, and on what, is a phone call rather than a query. Whether three degrees is the right conflict bar, what the real rubric is, and who the named tax adviser is are committee decisions the software is waiting on rather than questions it can answer. And if only one thing gets funded, it is the role and scope model: it is unglamorous, it produces no demo, and every other item on the list is harder or impossible without it.
