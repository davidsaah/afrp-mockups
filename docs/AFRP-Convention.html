★ The Convention Proposed

The Annual Convention is the federation’s largest single operation — governance floor, programme, banquet, store and help desk in one three-day burst — so it stands as its own element under AFRP programmes. Four surfaces: the member registration flow (frame 10 of the Unified Member document, built into the membership rails); the governance floor, where By-Law 9.1.3 weighted delegation runs on the certified roll frozen at the record date (frame 04 of Unified Operations); a participant layer — live schedule with stamped announcements, a small store for merchandise, banquet & excursion tickets and programme-book ads, and a help-desk form as the only two-way door; and an organizer backend whose club-integration seam reads each club’s own system where one exists and a dated manual roster where one does not. Store revenue posts gross through the existing journal rails under class CONV68.

04

Convention governance — weighted delegation, credentialing, quorum

In a contested convention a credentials committee has to explain why a delegate’s vote weighs what it weighs, not merely state it. By-Law 9.1.3 has no divisor, no rounding rule and no floor: each delegate independently casts the club’s certified count divided by the delegates it sent, and any certified member present may extract their own vote, re-dividing the pool. So the table shows the division in a column, exact to the hundredth, and the extraction panel shows the weight moving — Chicago’s 18.00 becoming 15.60 as twelve members walk up. Above it all sits the property everything else depends on: the certified roll was frozen at the record date and is never recomputed. Twenty-five people joined after 16 October and the house did not move.

Extraction — the weight moves

Any certified member present may cast their own vote; it is deducted from the delegation’s pool (9.1.3).

Chicago, certified5 delegates · 18.00 votes each90.00
12 members vote independentlyeach vote deducted from the pool−12.00
Delegate pool78.00
Each delegate now carries78.00 ÷ 5 — exact, in integer hundredths15.60
Chicago total voting power78.00 + 12.00 — capped at the certified count, By-Law 9.1.490.00
When is the weight fixed? The bylaws don’t say. Continuous re-division would let a member extracting mid-vote change the weight of ballots already cast — not survivable. The engine fixes weight at the opening of each question, extraction closing a stated interval before; the revision should say so in words.
Electronic voting is what makes this arithmetic practical: live on screen it is trivial, on paper it was miserable — which is why the first draft proposed replacing 9.1.3, and why that proposal was withdrawn.

What this screen decides

  • How many votes each delegate carries into the room, and whether the weight survives a challenge — with the division shown, not asserted.
  • Whether the convention can transact business at all: a flat 25 paid members present (10.1.5), met with room.

Rules made visible

  • FrozenThe certified roll is snapshotted at the record date into ballot_electorate and never recomputed. 25 later joiners changed nothing.
  • Exact decimalWeights are fractional and computed in exact decimal, never floating point — 90 ÷ 7 is not representable in binary, and a tally that fails to reconcile by a millionth of a vote is a tally somebody will litigate.
  • No floorThree certified members are three votes. A divisor scheme would put the three smallest clubs out of the house — a trade the drafting workbench quantifies before any vote adopts it, not after.

Detail notes

  • Club membership is not certification. Detroit has 41 club members, 33 current nationally, 26 certified — three different questions with three different answers.
  • The extraction re-division is why the credentialing screen shows a live weight rather than a printed seat count: every member who walks up moves it, and it fixes at question open.
  • Written proxies are absent from the bylaws; weighted delegation is the de facto proxy, and remote attendance counts as presence (10.3.1, “in person or otherwise”). Ties are frame 05’s subject: reported undecided, then a draw from a bag (9.2.4).

Club integration — two doors, one truth

Live feed. A club running the club system publishes its roster and dues state; the convention reads, never writes. Sync times on every row.
Roster upload. A club without one files a signed roster at the desk; it is dated, attributed, and marked manual wherever it is used.
Never a third door. Nobody edits a count on this screen. Corrections happen at the source and flow back — that is what makes the certified roll defensible in a contested floor fight.
C6

Store fulfilment & the desk — the participant screens, seen from behind the counter

Everything the participant touches on C1–C3 has exactly one operational home, and this is it. Orders become a pickup queue a badge scan can close; ticket capacity is watched where it is sold; the broadcast composer stamps and archives every announcement to the participant feed; and the help-desk queue turns the C3 form into owned, clocked work. Financially the store is boring on purpose — gross fees, class CONV68, straight into the daily close — because a convention that invents its own money handling is a convention the treasurer reconciles in January.
