The Bylaw Ruleset Workbench — Seven Screens

The screen the Constitution Committee uses to draft a revision and see what it would have done. The bylaws are data. The code implements mechanisms; the ruleset chooses among them and sets their parameters — and every change carries a cost badge that says which of the four tiers it falls into before anybody votes on it. The committee drafts bylaws-2027.1, runs it against the 2026 convention’s actual certified roll, actual delegations and actual motions, and reads back what would have changed: eight delegate seats, one club disenfranchised, one motion turned over and 340 members off the federal roll. Built on AFRP Design System v2, inlined verbatim — the same stylesheet, byte for byte, as Unified Operations and Unified Member.

Section A

The ruleset as data

The question this section asks: what is a ruleset, what may it contain, and what may never be changed? Two screens — the chain of versions with the immutability rule shown as a refusal rather than a paragraph, and the full four-tier inventory drawn from the actual documents.

01

Ruleset library — the chain of versions, and what relies on each

A ruleset is versioned data with three dates, not one: adopted, effective and superseded. Adoption is not effect — a revision carried at the July convention that takes effect with the new membership year on 1 September gives the office the intervening weeks to prepare and gives members notice. The column that does the real work here is what relies on it. bylaws-2012.2 has governed fourteen conventions, fifty-two ballots and fourteen certifications, and that is precisely why its edit control is disabled: changing it would change the answer to questions already decided. A correction to a ruleset something has relied on is a new version with a correction note, never an edit. And every entity holds its own chain — AFRP, ARFECF and each of the 26 Chapter Clubs — because By-Law 3.1 requires a club’s governing laws not to conflict with the Federation’s, which is a check the system can run at the moment a club rule is entered rather than at the moment it is disputed.

02

The four tiers — everything the bylaws set, sorted by what a change costs

“Make everything configurable” is a failure mode of its own: a system where anything can be changed is a system where nothing can be relied on, and a governance engine whose behaviour is entirely a matter of settings cannot certify anything. So the inventory has four categories, not one. Tier 1 is free. Tier 2 is a fork the system already implements on both sides, waiting for the ruleset to pick. Tier 3 is genuinely new behaviour and costs weeks — and saying so before the clause is drafted is the entire point. Tier 4 is not policy: it is the set of properties that make a result defensible, and it renders with no control at all. The design goal is to push work from 3 into 2 and from 2 into 1, and to be explicit about the boundary, so that when the committee proposes a clause the answer to “what does that cost us?” is known before the vote rather than after.

  • What a clause costs before it is drafted. The committee can see, at the moment of proposing, whether it is asking for a data edit or for six weeks of engineering.

Section B

Drafting

The question this section asks: what am I actually changing, and what does each change cost? One screen — the schedule in edit, with the by-law citation and the cost badge sitting next to every field, so that the price of a clause is visible at the moment it is written rather than at the moment it is implemented.

03

Editing a schedule — bylaws-2027.1 in draft, 59 parameters, 25 changed

The draft is a form, not a document. Fifty-nine parameters grouped by area, each showing the value in force, the proposed value where it differs, the by-law it comes from, and a cost badge that is a property of the engine rather than an opinion. Eighteen changes are green — a number, a date, a committee size — and take effect on the effective date with no release. Six are amber: mechanisms the system implements on both sides, needing to be enabled, tested and re-certified. One is red. Written proxies with assignment, limits, filing and revocation do not exist in the engine and do not exist in the by-laws; the field refuses the value and asks for an estimate before the clause is proposed, which is the honest answer and the useful one. Three of the amber changes — conjunctive standing, the threshold base and the abstention rule — are what produce every number on the next three screens.

04

Divergence — delegate weights and seats, club by club, against the 2026 convention

By-Law 9.1.3 as written is weighted, fractional delegate voting with member extraction: a club sends as many delegates as it likes and each carries certified ÷ delegates votes, so a club’s total voting power is simply its certified membership. There is no divisor, no rounding rule, no minimum club size and no cap. The proposed Schedule A replaces all of that with one delegate per twenty-five members or major fraction thereof, minimum one, maximum fifteen, and no delegate for a club of fewer than ten members. Run against the 2026 roll of 971 certified members across fourteen clubs, that turns 48 weighted delegates carrying 971.00 votes into 40 seats carrying 40.00 votes — and it takes San Jose’s seat away entirely. Chicago goes from 5 delegates carrying 18.00 votes each to 4 delegates carrying 1 vote each. The share column is the one to read: apportionment is not neutral, and the rounding rule quietly moves about a point of the room between clubs.

Non-retroactivity

The load-bearing clause. One sentence.

Every convention, ballot, apportionment, certification and disbursement stores the version that governed it, and is evaluated under that version forever. A 2026 election is a 2026 election in 2030. Without this, every amendment reopens every past decision and the versioned design has nothing to anchor to.

The Constitution does not currently say this. Article XI §1 revokes the previous Constitution “upon adoption”, with no effective date and no statement about decisions already made. It is the single most important thing the revision can add for the platform’s sake, and it is one sentence. Motion 2026-M2 on frame 05 is that sentence, carrying at 96.73%.
