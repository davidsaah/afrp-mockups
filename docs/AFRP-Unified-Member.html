My AFRP — the unified member experience

Twenty-one annotated phone screens covering every member-facing module of the AFRP platform in one document: membership and household, money, events and convention, governance, the family tree and life events, the directories, Hathihe Ramallah and the bookstore. All twenty-one follow one member — Nadia S. Khoury of the Detroit Club — through one continuous state of the world on 4 November 2026, so the rules are shown interacting rather than described one at a time. Every colour, size and component comes from AFRP Design System v2, inlined here verbatim; this document adds only device frames and annotations on top of it. The screens are live 375 px renderings, not drawings.

Decisions current as of August 2026: voting is conducted electronically — and the receipt on frame 13 proves that a ballot is in the box, never what it says; the dues ladder is a versioned schedule (dues-2026.1, figures provisional, the renamed Manar tier at $500 and Patron at $1,000); and the bylaws are being revised, with every governing rule held as versioned ruleset data — so the convention arithmetic on frames 10 and 18 follows By-Law 9.1.3’s weighted delegation.

The eight rules these twenty-one screens build to

Rule 1National and club are separate membershipsSeparate dues, separate cycles, independent lapse. There is no isInGoodStanding(person); only standingAt(scope, date). Every screen names its scope. Frames 01–04, 09–11, 18.
Rule 2Age sets defaults in a household, never rulesTurning 18 triggers a consent refresh, not an ejection, and an adult child may delegate authority back to a parent. Frames 03, 08.
Rule 3Eligibility is frozen at a record date, and an ineligible member is told whyPrecisely, with dates and a fix — never a hidden ballot. Frames 10, 11.
Rule 4A receipt proves inclusion, never content — and cannot be reissuedReissuing would require storing the voter→token link, which is the thing that makes the ballot secret; and a receipt that could show how she voted could be demanded, and therefore bought. Abstentions are counted and excluded from the denominator. Frames 12, 13.
Rule 5Living people are restricted; provisional records are shown as unverifiedA birth is always provisional and restricted, is never auto-published to social media, and a death needs family approval. Frames 14–16.
Rule 6Appearing in the magazine requires having told the systemMembers see exactly how they will appear in print and may correct or withdraw before the copy freeze. Silence still publishes. Frame 19.
Rule 7Directories are members-only and opt-in per fieldAbsence of a setting means not visible. RBPN contact details are never published; introductions are brokered. Frames 05, 17.
Rule 8Consent is per channel, with real opt-inNo pre-checked boxes anywhere, and SMS carries the full disclosure at the point of consent. Frames 05, 15, 16.

The member all twenty-one screens follow

Nadia S. Khoury, member no. 24 118, Detroit Club, of the Haddad clan. National Patron, active, $1,000 a year, auto-renewing 12 Mar 2027. Detroit Club Individual, $45 a year, lapsed 31 Aug 2026. A household of five: her husband Marwan (52), herself (55), Tarek (21, who has delegated renewals and registration back to her), Basim (20, who manages his own record entirely) and Layla (14, a minor). Three saved cards, one of them expiring next month. One active auto-renew subscription. She is in the federation electorate and not in the Detroit Club electorate — ineligible where she is lapsed, eligible where she is current — and both facts are on screen, because that is what happens the moment dual membership meets a frozen electorate. Every name, number, date and figure below is fictional. The organisation, its 26 clubs, the tiers, the programs, Hathihe Ramallah and the 68th Annual Convention are real.

Frames 01–05

A · Membership & household

Two memberships, two dues, two renewal dates and two independent lapse states — and a household of five in which age sets defaults and nothing else. Every screen in this group names the scope it is talking about, because “is she a member in good standing?” has no answer until you say where.

Frames 06–07

B · Money

What she has given, to what, and how she gives again. Dues are not gifts and purchases are not gifts; the receipt says so before an accountant has to.

Frames 08–10

C · Events & convention

One calendar for both scopes, one registration flow for a whole household, and the convention as its own destination — where the club lapse stops being an abstraction and starts costing $15 and a delegate seat.

Frames 11–13

D · Governance

Three screens, four promises: a ballot is never hidden from the member it excludes; a secret vote cannot be changed; a receipt proves that a ballot is in the box — never what it says — and cannot be reissued; and an abstention is counted but never dilutes the threshold. The ballot is cast electronically: the double envelope of By-Law 9.2.2 becomes a digital separation of identity from choice, and the CPA’s sealed box becomes a key split three ways.

Frames 14–16

E · Family & life events

The archive and the announcement are the same object seen twice. A living person is restricted, an unmoderated record is provisional, a birth is both, and a death waits for the family — in the tree, in the app, and in the magazine.

Frames 17–18

F · Community

The two places a member goes to find other people: the directory, which is members-only and opt-in per field, and the club, which is where most of AFRP actually happens.

Frames 19–20

G · Magazine & bookstore

Hathihe Ramallah is the forcing function: three times a year it gives every member a reason to tell the system the truth about themselves. The bookstore is the other half — member pricing, and digital goods held as entitlements rather than emailed as links.

Frame 21

H · Programmes

Seventeen programmes on afrp.org are one pipeline from childhood to legacy, and a household moves along it without re-entering its data. This is the pipeline from Nadia’s side of the glass — including the place where her own club lapse reaches her daughter.

What these twenty-one screens commit AFRP to

Six of the things on this page are promises rather than decoration. Each has a cost, and the cost is the reason it is worth writing down before build rather than discovering it in month four.

CommitmentEvery standing answer names a scope and a dateWhich means no screen, receipt, report or email may ever say “member in good standing” unqualified — including the ones officers write themselves. Frames 01, 02, 09, 10, 11, 18.
CommitmentAn ineligible member sees the ballot and the reasonWhich means the eligibility engine must return a reason string good enough to show a member, for every rejection path, not just the common one. Frames 10, 11.
CommitmentSupport can never recover a receiptWhich means staff need a scripted answer, and the screen has to carry the warning hard enough that the call never happens. Frame 13 is that screen.
CommitmentAbsence of a consent is a decision, not a gapWhich means every consent-bearing field needs a tri-state in the schema — granted, refused, never asked — and the UI has to render the third one honestly. Frames 03, 05, 15, 17.
CommitmentNothing reaches print that the member was not shown firstWhich means the proof-and-confirm run is a hard dependency of every issue, two weeks before every copy freeze, forever. Frame 19.
CommitmentA birth is never posted, and a death waits for the familyWhich means there is no admin override for either, at any role, and the publishing pipeline has to be able to say no to an officer. Frames 15, 16.

Still open

The bylaws have now been read against the design, and the governance values here follow them: weighted delegation at certified ÷ delegates sent (By-Law 9.1.3, with the 9.1.4 cap), a flat Annual Meeting quorum of 25 paid members (10.1.5), two-thirds delegate vote for amendments (Const. XI §2), ties drawn from a bag (9.2.4), and electronic voting in place of 9.2’s mailed procedure — all held as versioned ruleset data, so a revision is a data change with a divergence report, never a rebuild. Still genuinely open: whether Assembly abstentions sit in or out of the denominator, per motion type; postmark versus receipt for the 28 February dues test (5.3 vs 8.15.5); whether complimentary memberships vote (8.15.4); and whether the wedding benefit should also cover club dues.

Three product questions are genuinely open and are not resolved by anything above: how many issues of Hathihe Ramallah a year and what the real press dates are, since every magazine deadline keys off them; whether back issues are a member benefit or open to the public; and whether a club may set its own dues amount or must use a federation schedule, which decides whether frame 18's remittance card is one number or twenty-six.
