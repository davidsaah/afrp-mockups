The seven settlements · three red-team rounds

The platform principles, S1–S7

Everything below obeys these. They were not designed up front — each one was forced by a confirmed break and then generalized, which is why they simplify the platform exactly where it failed and nowhere else.

S1 Verified channel first

No money, no PII, no consent across an unverified channel. Join, login and the 18th-birthday refresh are one front door.

S2 One life-event state machine

Claimed → verified-pending → released-per-surface. Operational effects fire at verified-pending, reversibly; publications wait for the gate.

S3 One reversal model

Receivables across remitted or entity boundaries; effective-dated standing; frozen snapshots annotated, never recomputed.

S4 Weights fix when voting opens

Floor extraction re-divides between motions only; club-less members count through an at-large roll. Both drafted as bylaw amendments.

S5 Corrections held, never lost

A dispute blocks the entry, not the issue; archives ingest only consent-classed material; adopted records are append-only, corrected by new entries.

S6 Refusals name the rule, not the relatives

Conflict blocks never leak the tree; allocations are not restrictions and the words say which is which.

S7 Suppression must survive composition

Aggregates publish as frozen snapshots with complementary suppression — a cell shows only when the cell AND its complement clear the floor, across tabs, time and public surfaces.

Four entities, one administration

The entity map

The platform administers all four without merging any of them. Money reports per entity; scope rules end at each entity’s edge; crossings ride standing data-sharing agreements.

I · The front door

The piece the platform never had: how a person becomes a member, signs in, recovers, and comes of age — all under S1.

Public landing Designed

Public, member-data-free, and honest: what membership is, the real 18 clubs, real programs, dues tiers under the named ruleset. Exactly two doors — Join and Sign in. Signed-out pages never reveal whether any person is a member.

S1 · R8-closed

Landing

Join — the seven-step wizard · now a working simulation Designed

Payment is last, on purpose. A tier choice creates only a 30-day, PII-minimal draft; email and mobile verify by code before anything else is stored; then identity & lineage (optional, provisional), household (an adult of the target household accepts), clubs (0..N, each its own record), consent defaults (marketing OFF, operational ON, program invitations ON — the Pathways class, directory OFF), and one gross-fee checkout — which also collects the postal address recovery and the mailed ballot both need — with national revenue and club liability on separate line groups.

The typo attack. omar@gmali.com dies at step 2 with nothing stored, charged, or mailed. An abandoned draft is contacted at most once, on the channel that started verifying.

The family branch. A household joining together (including the newlywed Family tier) is N wizards sharing one checkout: each adult verifies their OWN channels before their record exists; consent steps are per person; minor records are created via the consent-holder, with no credentials.

Walk it yourself. The prototype’s join screen is a fully working in-memory simulation of every path — individual end-to-end, the newlywed family branch with a minor, the $0 student tier, and the unhappy moments (a mistyped email, an expired code, a declined household request) handled as gracefully as the happy ones. Nothing is stored or charged; reload and it’s gone.

Sign in, sessions & passkeys Designed

Passwordless: a one-time code to any verified channel, with one constant reply — “If this channel is on a record, a code is on its way.” Same wording, same timing, account or not: absence is not signal, applied to authentication. Optional per-device passkeys after first sign-in; a sessions screen lists every device and revokes any within a minute. Every credential change notifies every verified channel, with undo windows.

S1 · R18 · J15-absence

Sign in

Recovery — deliberately slow Designed

Lost one channel: sign in with the other, add a verified replacement, and notice goes to ALL channels — including the one replaced — with a 72-hour undo. Lost both: a support-desk case with identity evidence, a 7-day delay, and a mailed notice to the postal address on file before any credential moves. No postal address on file? A slower path: in-person or notarized evidence and a longer delay — never a faster one. A household adult never recovers for another adult; a delegate never signs in as you — delegation excludes credentials.

Staff, officers & partners Designed

The front door authenticates more than members: staff, club officers, program leads and third parties (the CPA) get sponsored accounts on the same passwordless and passkey rails — each one a (person, role, scope) grant, provisioned by role, de-provisioned when the role ends, and scoped to its one surface: the CPA sees the mailed-ballot roll export and the scan-return channel, and nothing else.

R39 · S1

Roles & permissions

Minors & the 18th-birthday refresh Designed

Under 16: no credentials — the record exists, interaction surfaces through the household. 16–17: sign-in with the consent-holder’s co-consent, in a flagged minor session (no payments, no directory, no consent changes beyond her own program forms). In the 90 days before the 18th birthday the system asks the minor — at program touchpoints — to verify a channel of her own; on the birthday the consent refresh goes there, never to a parent-supplied channel. If no channel was ever verified by the birthday, the record stays active but consent-gated actions and publications hold until she claims it through the front door — a defined state, not a void.

II · Membership, household & money

One person, one identity; N memberships, each its own record; money that posts gross and reverses by rule.

Identity, household & consent Built

National and club memberships are separate peer records, never merged (R1/R2). One person, one identity; merges go through a queue, never automatically (R3). A household registers together but is never one consent — each adult owns their own record (R4); minors never appear in any directory (R7). Consent is per channel, visibility per field (R27/R28); delegation is explicit, dated, revocable, scoped — and excludes credentials (R6).

III · Clubs

The real eighteen, as first-class entities the federation reads but never edits.

Club registry, setup & health Designed

The registry is the single list — 18 real clubs with status, officers, integration door, remittance schedule, certified count. Setup is five steps: charter → officers → money → integration door → electorate; forming → active is a Board action. Two doors, one truth: a live club-system feed is data, the remittance ledger is truth for standing (R17/R42); disagreement lands in an exception queue. Certified counts are club-attested AND ledger-verified, deduplicated against club-of-record before any weight computes.

Dormancy. Effective-dated; members keep memberships until each expires; multi-club members redesignate club of record; only the club-less fall to the at-large roll. Prepaid dues stay a liability — remitted on reactivation or refunded via S3.

R17 · R42 · R16 · S4

Registry & setupClub healthJourney J14

Onboarding & migration wizard Designed

“Which club goes first” — the club decides, always. Five stages: the club chooses; full import with identity resolution (duplicates to the merge queue, never auto-merged; nothing written back; club-only people become club-membership records with provisional identity); reconciliation to the cent with the treasurer; one-way nightly delta sync, pausable indefinitely; a cutover vote under the club’s own rules; legacy retired to read-only archive. Reversible until cutover; the labelled door never changes silently; an electorate frozen mid-migration reads the door as of its record date. Cutover adds the registry’s third door value — platform-native, effective-dated — which credentialing and the convention read first-class.

Verification outreach before cutover. Imported emails and phones are UNVERIFIED channels, and S1 forbids money or consent across them. Before the cutover vote, each member verifies a channel front-door-style; the unverified land on a hygiene queue with postal fallback. Billing stays with the club’s own system through stage 3 — the platform bills only what it has always billed nationally, until cutover.

IV · Governance & voting

Frozen electorates, receipts that prove inclusion never content — and now the flow current law actually requires.

The mailed CPA ballot Designed

Both the 2012 text in force and the 2024 text keep the mailed CPA secret ballot; the platform’s authority ends at the frozen roll and the envelope IDs — ballot content never enters it. Record-date snapshot → address hygiene (no valid address still means ON the roll, surfaced) → watermarked expiring roll export to the CPA, logged → double-envelope mail (outer envelope IDs the voter for roll-checking; inner is anonymous) → member sees mailed/received for their own row only → received-or-postmarked deadline named per ruleset → the CPA counts; the platform imports certified totals and the certification artifact.

Exceptions. Duplicate returns: first validly received counts, later logged unopened. Address fixed after export: exception queue to the CPA, re-mail before the cutoff (a Board-set date, named on screen) only — the export is never regenerated, and a re-mail VOIDS the original envelope ID before a new one issues, so two live IDs never exist.

Status, not receipt. The member panel is live inclusion STATUS — their own row, mailed/received — not a receipt. The one-time, non-reissuable receipt of R14 belongs to the electronic future-amendment flow.

The scan-return channel. Envelope-ID scans come back from the CPA as signed daily batch files validated against the frozen roll; scans for IDs not on the roll are rejected and logged. Disputes get a stage: retention named per ruleset, a recount produces a SECOND certification artifact imported append-only — superseding, never editing, the first (S5).

The electronic flow. Everything electronic in this prototype is a future-amendment design and is labelled as such on every screen.

Board & committee workspace Designed

The system of record between conventions. Motions: draft → seconded → discussion → vote → resolution, append-only once adopted (S5). Minutes approved at the next meeting, then append-only. The 48 provisional overrides form a ratification queue, each linking to the workbench divergence view. Action items escalate to the next agenda when stale. Committees get the same rails scoped down — one design, one audit — and the separate legal entities get entity-scoped boardrooms on those rails (the ARFHSN BOT is not an AFRP committee; the ARFECF board’s roster feeds the interlock view): AFRP-side motions link across, confidential documents never cross the entity line.

Board-confidential is a grant, not a scope. R39’s federation scope satisfies club and program for reads — so a “board” scope cannot exist. Board-confidential is an exact-grant class, the sixth sensitive permission: no scope, including federation, reads it by inheritance.

What members share with each other, gated by consent at every surface.

The directory & RBPN Designed

IX · Member operations — inbox & support

The connective tissue: every notice explained, every problem one door away.

Notifications centre Designed

Every notice the platform sent, with the consent class that authorized it — operational, marketing, or program invitation (the Pathways class). Mute by class; marketing mutes immediately and audited; operational cannot be muted away. Deleting an entry never unsends or edits the audit. Relay content stays between the two members — staff see that a relay happened, never what it said. Bounces feed the hygiene queues the mailed-ballot roll reads.

Member support desk Designed

The convention help desk made year-round: one two-way door, owned and clocked, SLAs visible to the member.
