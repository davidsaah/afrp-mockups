Why this can flip

By-Law 4.3.1 as written makes good standing conjunctive — AFRP and local club dues current. Held that way, your lapsed Detroit membership costs you your federal vote. Held as independent per scope, your national Patron standing is enough. The ruleset chooses; the engine only obeys.

Four promises

  1. A ballot is never hidden from the member it excludes — the refusal names its rule and version.
  2. A secret vote cannot be changed after sealing, and no interim tally exists for anyone.
  3. A receipt proves inclusion only — never content, so it cannot be sold or coerced.
  4. A receipt cannot be reissued — that would require storing the voter→token link, which is never stored.

The rules on this ballot

  1. One choice only. A ballot showing a vote for more than one candidate is disqualified (9.2.5) — here the interface prevents the over-vote instead of punishing it.
  2. A blank is a real choice. On paper you could decline to mark the ballot; removing that option would quietly abolish a choice the by-laws allowed. A blank ballot and a non-voter are distinguishable in the record.
  3. Plurality wins, irrespective of the percentage of votes or the number of candidates (9.2.4).
  4. A tie is drawn from a bag under the Election Committee Chairman — not by a random number generator. The system reports undecided and hands it to the chair.

What sealing means

Your identity is checked at the door, then separated from the vote — the digital equivalent of the double envelope in 9.2.2, where the outer envelope carried identity and the inner carried the vote. After sealing, the voter→ballot link is never stored, which is why a lost receipt cannot be reissued and a cast vote cannot be shown to anyone.

Sealed to a key split three ways: any two can open, no one alone can — not staff, not the Executive Director, not a database administrator. The audit trail records who opened, when, and with which two keys. At the count the agency attests to the box: how many ballots, seal intact, roll matching the certification.

No interim tally exists to show. This is not a permission — the data does not exist until opening. The system is incapable of a partial result, not merely configured against one.

The integrity floor — locked

Not policy. Not configurable, at any price.

  1. The voter↔ballot link is never stored. No setting exposes it.
  2. No interim tallies exist — for anyone, including administrators and testing.
  3. Frozen rolls are stored, never recomputed at tally time.
  4. Every decision records its ruleset version; every refusal names its rule.
  5. Ties report undecided. The audit trail is append-only. Vote arithmetic is exact decimal.

If the revision ever requires one of these to bend, that is a conversation, not a configuration change — and it should be a loud one.
